Community Mentoring Framework

Mentoring is a powerful tool for restoration, but it requires clear boundaries. This framework outlines the role of a Beloved Berachah community mentor.

The Role of a Mentor

A mentor is a compassionate listener and a reliable guide. A mentor is not a licensed therapist, legal advisor, or crisis worker. Your goal is to walk alongside, not to "fix".

Establishing Boundaries

  • Communication: Define exactly when and how you can be contacted (e.g., "I am available by text between 9 AM and 5 PM").
  • Commitment: Be consistent. If you promise to call at a specific time, follow through. Broken promises can mirror past traumas.
  • Confidentiality: Maintain strict privacy, except in cases where safeguarding policies require you to report a risk of harm.

Active Listening Techniques

Focus on the "WAIT" principle: Why Am I Talking? Allow the mentee to guide the conversation. Use affirming responses ("I hear you," "That sounds incredibly difficult") rather than immediate solutions.
